Project Write-up

Motivation

Our project asks how living conditions and development changed across countries from 1960 to 2020. We explored five indicators: internet use, electricity access, research and development spending, fertility, and population aging. These indicators show different aspects of development, including access to technology, infrastructure, investment, and demographic change. Together, the charts let viewers explore changes over time and differences between countries and income groups.

Design Decisions and Interactions

For internet use, we chose grouped bars to compare income groups around 2000 and 2019. Placing the bars beside each other makes the differences easy to compare. The vertical axis runs from 0% to 100%, while light and dark blue distinguish the earlier and later periods. Hovering reveals the exact percentage and observation year.

For electricity access, we used two world maps with the same color scale to show geographic differences in 2000 and 2019. Light cream represents lower access, dark purple represents higher access, and gray represents missing data. Country tooltips provide exact values. Synchronized zooming and panning help viewers examine smaller countries while keeping both years aligned.

We used line charts for R&D spending and fertility because both views focus on change over time. Hovering over an R&D point reveals its year, average expenditure, and number of reporting countries or economies. On the fertility chart, viewers can drag across a period to zoom in and double-click to return to the full range. Its vertical scale stays fixed during zooming, and a dashed line provides a reference at approximately 2.1 births per woman.

For population aging, we combined box plots with individual country dots. Each box summarizes the middle 50% of observations, and its median line shows the center of the distribution. The dots preserve country differences that a summary alone would hide. Hovering reveals country values, while brushing highlights selected countries across all four years. Orange separates selected countries from the blue background points.

Data Preparation and Limitations

We used World Bank indicators from the dataset linked in the assignment. Data preparation selects the relevant indicators and years, removes missing or invalid observations, and saves smaller CSV files for the charts. Country comparisons exclude regional and income-group aggregates. The internet chart intentionally uses income-group aggregates and a World comparison, while fertility uses the World Bank's World series.

Coverage differs between indicators. The overall research question spans 1960–2020, while each view uses the available observations: fertility covers 1960–2019; population aging compares 1960, 1980, 2000, and 2019; electricity compares 2000 and 2019; and R&D covers 1996–2018. For internet use, we selected the closest available observation within three years of each target year. The low-income comparison therefore uses 2017 instead of 2019.

R&D values are equally weighted averages of countries and economies with available observations. Reporting coverage changes between years, so changes in the average can partly reflect differences in coverage. Missing electricity observations appear in gray. These views describe patterns across different indicators; they do not establish causal relationships between them.

Development Process

We began with five exploratory charts and then added interactions. Our group discussed connecting country selection on the world map to the fertility chart. The final version uses navigation buttons to bring the five views together under one research question while keeping their different measures and scales readable.

Juan contributed the electricity maps and the R&D and population-aging charts, including their data preparation. Anuradha completed the fertility brush zoom and chart navigation buttons.
